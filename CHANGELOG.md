## 3.19.0

*Released 31 October 2026.*

- **Family lines**: *Tree overview* has a *Family lines* section: every family line of the tree (the birth line along the male line, named after its founder) with its years, members by generation and married-in spouses, a search and an order; the detail of a line shows it in the tree and *Edit family line…* corrects it (another founder, a separate line, name forms and surname spellings, back to the automatic line), with a preview of each change before it is applied. Families with too few people for a line of their own are listed as *Other families*.
- **Family line of a person**: the person card and the person page have a *Family line* row with a link to the line in the tree overview and a menu: show the line in the tree, edit it, start a family line from this person, the line's book.
- **Family book**: a new output, a book of chosen family lines, of the whole tree or of a person's ancestors or descendants. The options choose the parts, the detail per person, notes and researcher data, the privacy of living people, the language and the title, show an estimate of the pages and are kept per tree. The book opens in a window with its contents at the side, *Save as HTML*, *Print* and *Change options*, on a phone as well.
- **Person page**: under each register excerpt a link *Scan in the archive* leads to its scan, at the event and in the source card, in the window and in the saved file. On the map close places join into one numbered cluster with one label; the gallery shows a register entry or a document whole on a paper ground, a wide one over two columns; on a wide screen the picture blocks take the whole column beside the contents. The map's clusters take in the children's and partners' places too, a dot of the world inset is clickable like the main map, and a wide page gives the children's row of the family tree its whole width.
- **Places**: the dialog sorts the places *By name*, *By number of people* or *Not on the map first*.
- **History in the person card**: a region not searched yet and the place's own history are offered together (*Find the history of …* · *Find local history…*); a range of years no longer widens the year column.
- **Kinds of history**: each event of history shows its kind as a symbol (war, epidemic, census, state and borders, emigration, law, economy, disaster, other) in the timeline, in *History of places* and on the person page; a collapsed year names its kinds with small symbols and *What the marks mean* shows all nine. On the person page the symbols stand on the history line and in the life chart, coloured by their origin, with a row naming the kinds shown; the person's own rows carry the marks of the person card.
- **Timeline**: the focused person's history runs in a row under the axis of years, as kind symbols, close years joined with a count; a click opens the person card at that year, on a phone the events show in a list.
- **Tree from a newer version**: a tree saved by a newer version of the app opens read-only, at the start and when switched to; a bar says why and that updating the app makes it editable again, and nothing is written into it.
- **Research menu**: *Other actions* come in a new order, *Update the research* right after loading a new version. Where the research offers bulk fixes of the data, the menu has *Tree fixes* with the count of the available ones; each fix shows its count (in people, families, events or sources), a short description and its state, and opens or is undone in the research's terminal. *Research tools* lists the research's own tools grouped by what they do, the research check first. *Written to the research* seen once no longer comes back at every start, and a removal the research only noted says that it can be deleted in the research.
- **Research mark**: actions that open the research's terminal end with its mark instead of ↗, which stays for the web only.
- **Smaller changes**: the research's version date is the local day of its time; when reading a document, a difference between the readers in a person's role, name or presence shows at that person; the family book keeps the sources of the deceased under the initials privacy; the new-version prompt waits while a dialog is open; a phone held sideways at 640 px or less shows the tree right under the header; further fixes of layout and texts.
- **Data**: data version 14 (the tree keeps the corrections of its family lines); trees of earlier versions open without a change.

## 3.18.0

*Released 30 October 2026.*

- **Person page**: the header of the card menu (and a button in the phone's sheet) opens *Person page*, a read-only page of one person's life: dates in words, family, the story and the couple's stories (a draft not yet approved marked as one), notes, a life timeline with history and children's events (both switchable), the main register excerpts and, folded away, the sources and a section *For researchers*. Relatives open in the same window with *Back*. *Save as HTML* makes one self-contained file that opens offline in any browser, also without JavaScript, to send to family; the content groups and the privacy of living people can be chosen (initials and birth year by default; it hides the names, dates, sources and images of living people only, the deceased keep theirs) and the choice is remembered. Print and, on a phone, Share are there too.
- **Person page, life in pictures**: under the header a summary *In brief* (age at death, marriages, children, the longest occupation), then the family as a small family tree (a rail on a phone), the story with the couple's stories as folded chapters, the open question, *Places of life* on a map drawn offline (Natural Earth outlines, a world inset for places far away, the children's places switchable, *Open in map*), a life chart above the text timeline (youth, marriages, places lived, the partners' and children's lives), one gallery of excerpts, photos and attachments (also in a tree without the research) and *People around* (godparents, witnesses). Places in the header and the timeline lead to the map, the chart's marks to their timeline rows. The saved file has all of it, opens images in a light box and starts with the switches as the window showed them; *Save as HTML* offers *Attach images* and *Attach PDF documents* (both on by default) with the expected file size, and large images go in reduced. A protected file leaves living people out of every new block; only the family tree shows them, as initials and the birth year. The *Story* window has a chip for each chapter when there are more, a tree card with only a couple's story has a hollow corner, the menu item counts the chapters and the family book gives a couple's story its own subchapter. The saved file can carry the contents *On this page* (on by default); places close to each other get a map of their neighbourhood with distance rings, the nearest town and the region; the family tree, the timeline and the places keep the precision of dates (*about 1798*, *before 1965*); the labels of history in the life chart no longer overlap; and printing gives the whole page.
- **Couple's stories**: the *Story* window tells a couple's story under the person's own, one per marriage with its title or the two names, a draft marked as one, with *Approve in the research ↗* beside it, and a new version ready to compare. The card menu offers *Story* and the person card shows the *Story* section, and the tree card its mark, also when there is only a couple's story.
- **Surname in use**: a person with a married surname can go by the birth surname again (for example after a divorce): the person editor has the choice *Surname in use: Birth | Married* under the filled married surname. The married surname stays; the card and the tree show only the surname in use, and the person's detail (card menu, relationships panel, editor header) says the other one as *(formerly Zástěrová)*. The choice travels through GEDCOM (`_STROM_PRIMARY`), JSON and the research link, the CSV export has a *Surname in use* column and the family book index says *formerly*. Trees without the choice are unchanged.
- **Fixes**: a child of a person with two or more partners is linked to both parents: the line comes down from the middle between the two parents' cards for every partner, with or without a marriage and in any order of the families. Until now the child of a second partner was linked to that partner's card alone.

## 3.17.0

*Released 29 October 2026.*

- **Read a document**: the end of a reading is announced once: a start of the app no longer brings *Document read* or *The document can’t be read* again for readings that ended long ago, and of a document read more than once only the newest reading says how it ended, also in the menu and the panel.
- **Installing the research on a Mac**: under the line a note says that a newer macOS may stop the paste with *Possible malware, Paste blocked*, that the line from the Strom app installs Strom Research from GitHub (ACiDekCZ/strom-research) and that *Paste Anyway* pastes it, with Apple's page about the message; also under the line for a Mac sent from a phone.
- **Links to a tree**: a link that names a research (*?research=…*) opens the tree tied to that research, also among several trees of one name; a research not known here leaves the choice to the tree's name as before.
- **History in the life timeline** (on by default, can be switched off in Settings under *Person card*): the person card's timeline shows events of the time and of the places where the person lived, with the person's age then, found by the research and each with its source (without such events the card stays as it was); the marks are explained under *What the marks mean*. An event can be hidden for a place or for one person only, with *Undo*, and the *Hidden* tab of *History of places* shows it again. Settings also says how far the research's search is, and when the research's version does not offer history yet.
- **History of places**: a dialog opened from Settings under *Person card*, the *Research* menu, the legend and the person card shows the regions and places whose history the research looks for, with their state and a search, in the tabs *Regions*, *Places*, *Hidden* and *Deleted*. A region's detail lists its events with filters; a region can be searched first (*Find first*), all its events hidden (*Hide all*) or left out (*Do not search*), a place can be given its region by hand, and an event can be deleted and restored when the research's version offers it. Settings under *Person card* switch the kinds *Regional history*, *Local history* and *From research sources* separately, and the person card's timeline offers *Find the history of …* for a region not searched yet. When the research's version searches the two apart, the dialog's header shows *Regional history* and *Local history* each on or off: one on can be turned off there, one off is turned on in the research; *Find first* needs *Regional history* on and *Find local history…* needs *Local history* on. The *Places* tab keeps every period of a region (a state 1918–1938 and again 1945–1992), says the state of the region and of the place's own history together, and lists a place that differs only by a street under its municipality.
- **History in stories**: *Research for this tree* has a *Stories* section with *History in stories*: the research may then use the history of places as a source when writing stories; finished stories are not rewritten for it.
- **Switching on the search for history**: the research looks for the history of places only once its search is switched on in the research (when it is set up or in its Settings), or with *Turn on the search in the research* in Settings under *Person card*. Until then the person card's timeline of a tree tied to the research says so in one line with that link; × closes the line for good in this browser.
- **Tree validation**: the findings are split into *Problems* and *Leads*, with one summary sentence and groups by type; leads are not errors but hints for the research. A recurring godparent is a lead only when found in more than one family, and never the priest or the midwife. A finding can be marked *Not an error*, a possible duplicate *Two different people*, with *Undo*; marked findings are listed apart and *Restore* reports them again. The marks stay with the tree, also in a JSON export, and the *Tree overview* counts the same way.
- **Person card timeline**: *Tree settings* has *Person card timeline – default*, where each tree sets whether the timeline first shows *Children's events* and *History*; the switches on a card change it for that card only. An event of history can be hidden *in this whole tree* or *for one person only*. The kinds *Regional history*, *Local history* and *From research sources* can differ per tree: Settings give every tree its default, the tree's settings keep only the kinds this tree shows otherwise, and the card's legend says *Off for this tree*.
- **Places**: the dialog lists the places by name in the order of the app's language, has a search that needs no diacritics and the chip *No location* for the places not on the map.
- **What was written**: the research's last write is news only until it is seen; once its list has been opened, it does not come back after the research restarts or is updated.
- **Periods in dates**: a period *from … to …* (GEDCOM *FROM … TO …*, also *from …* or *to …* alone) stays a period: it is shown as *from 22 Mar 1871 to 22 Mar 1882* and goes out as it came, apart from *between … and …*. Changes sent to the research leave untouched dates and ages exactly as the research wrote them (*CAL 1885*, *EST 1842*, *0y 2m 26d*), so they no longer show there as edits.
- **Surname spellings**: *Suggest from the research* lists which spellings the research finds belonging together, documented ones apart from those by the rules, each with its reason and evidence; a suggestion can be accepted or rejected, with *Undo*, and its first record in sight is one with a different spelling.
- **Data**: data version 13 (the tree keeps the findings marked as not an error and the rejected spelling suggestions); trees of earlier versions open without a change.

## 3.16.0

*Released 25 October 2026.*

- **Materials**: the family's files kept in the Strom archive or the research appear beside the tree's attachments: a *Materials* section in the person's editor, *Materials of …* in the card menu and a window of all materials and batches, with a viewer, assigning to people, hiding and restoring; *Download* saves a research file under its name, hiding one file names it, and long file names wrap between their parts.
- **Children in place**: the children tab of a card and *Show children here* show a person's children right where the card is, and a second click hides them again; the view stays. The other cards glide aside while the children grow out of their parents' line, and the shown children's cards wear the hatch of cards linked in the view only, until their parent's children are hidden again. The tab is offered only where showing the children draws someone new, also through partner chains of several people. An ancestor's children stand beside their sibling on the direct line, under their parents, and no card covers another; ancestors in the way are folded for the moment under a dashed *▸ parents* mark, whose click hides the children and brings the ancestors back. A child drawn elsewhere in the tree is named and reached from the card. *Hide shown · n* hides every expansion at once and returns the view as it was. An ancestor's other partners stand beside the ancestor, over their own children, and in the expanded view a child of a second marriage stands under that husband's line. The expanded view now also draws the children of every family in a chain of partners running through several people. The partner of a shown child shows their own children too. Showing or hiding children, *Hide shown · n* and the *▸ parents* mark are steps of back and forward (↩ ↪), and back returns the view as it was before the step. A jump to a card elsewhere (*child ↗*, *parents ↗*, a child drawn elsewhere) glides there, lands with the card readable and is a step of back and forward too.
- **Tabs on the cards**: a card's tabs show by their look what a click does: round *▸ / ▾* show and hide relatives in place, square *◎* sets a new focus (*Focused on … | ↩ Back* says so and leads back), and a word with *↗* moves the view to cards drawn elsewhere. *▸ siblings* shows in place the children of both parents from all their marriages, and a sibling standing elsewhere in the tree is named under *Already in the tree elsewhere*; the aunts and uncles it brings come with their partners, and a person married more than once to one partner stays with that partner in the standard view. The parents' siblings stand beside the parent instead of beyond the whole width of the family, with no long line across the tree. An ancestor at the edge of the depth has *▸ parents*, which shows its parents alone; persons married in keep *◎ family*. *▸ ∞ N* shows a person's other partners beside the card and offers every marriage out of view, with the number of children of each, in the standard and the expanded view; a click on a name in its bubble brings that partner alone, *▸ ∞ n* then brings the rest, and the person's menu offers the same names under *∞ Other partners*. A tab's bubble stands over the tree and the zoom buttons at one size at any zoom, always whole in the window; it names eight people at most, then *and n more*, and fewer in a low window. By touch a finger held on a tab opens its bubble below the tab, or above it where only that fits, never over the bottom bar and always with at least one name, else the person's menu opens; when zoomed far out the whole tab opens the person's menu, as on the card, and the menu starts with *↗ Go to …* for each of the card's jumps. A bubble that by touch leaves something out says *More in the person menu*. A message coming while a bubble is open stands above it, and a bubble opened over a message hides it until the bubble closes.
- **Strom archive**: the tree switcher tells an *Archive* from a *Research*, changes go into an archive by themselves (the recommended choice there), and a research waiting for its agent is shown as an archive until the agent starts.
- **Writes held back**: when the research cannot write yet (*Sealed on another computer*, *Files not downloaded yet*, *No room left on the disk*), its own sentence is shown, the changes wait here unsent and nothing is sent again by itself; once the research says it is settled, the block goes away.
- **Another window**: a person removed by a send from another window is said as *Removed in another window*, with the research's version offered; whether the person comes back or goes is asked once, and once decided, the research's version loads without asking when nothing here is unsent.
- **Sending**: changes that go by themselves are sent when the page is left, and changes waiting while the research is not running are counted; a write the research itself makes while it is followed no longer brings an offer of a needlessly newer version, and a change of its agent is not lost.
- **Messages on a phone** take the phone's width and wrap a long sentence, with their action whole under it, and cover neither the back and forward buttons, the zoom buttons nor the bottom bar, not even while they come and go; on a tablet they no longer slide across the bottom bar either. The tour offer steps aside while a message is shown. Only one message stands at a time, the *Undo* note included: a new one takes its place and keeps its *Undo* or *↩ Back* for a while, and a message with an action stands at least 3 seconds before another replaces it. On a phone a message stands between the back and forward buttons and the zoom buttons, or above them where its sentence does not fit there, so the *Undo* note never ends in “…”; on a phone held sideways *Focused on …* stands at the bottom again.
- **Fixes**: the hint and the example under *Other spellings of the name* use neutral sample spellings (*Brauschek*, *Prousek*), and the English example names an English farm instead of a Czech one. The small text on a hatched card reads clearly in both themes, and the dark hatch is fainter. In Czech, undoing a change is now called *Vrátit*, and *Zpět* stays for the return to the previous view.

## 3.14.0

*Released 21 October 2026.*

- **Read a document**: the *Research* menu has *Read a document…*, and the person menu *Read a document for this person…*. The research agent reads a photo, scan or PDF (several files are pages of one document) and gives a transcription, a translation and an explanation, with the entries framed on the page and illegible spots marked. One reader is faster and cheaper; two independent readers are more reliable and show where they differ. The window can be closed while reading continues, and the result is announced. Reading needs research 1.18.0 or newer with the Claude agent, and runs on the agent's own subscription.
- **Suggest for the tree**: from a reading, each person can be added as new, linked to a person in the tree, or left out; a linked person gains only what is new, and a differing value is kept as another form. Events and relationships can be ticked, and a second reader can read the document before anything is written. Nothing is added without confirmation, and one Undo step takes back the people, the source with the transcription, the crop of the entry and the citations. Grandparents, godparents and other relatives named in the document can be linked too, each with the likeliest person in the tree already picked, found also through the relatives linked beside them; someone in the tree who died before the person was born, baptised or married is not picked. A link with nobody chosen says so on its card with *Choose…* and writes nothing until a person is chosen.
- **Read documents**: *Research for this tree…* (in the *Research* menu under *Other actions*) lists the research's read documents, newest first, and each opens its result again. A source written from a reading has *Reading result…* in its detail and in the person's sources, opening the result on the source's own entry. When the research cannot show it (not running, an older version, an archive), a short note says why and the source's own transcription is shown, ready to copy.
- **Phone on the home network**: on a computer, the *Research* menu of a tree with research has *Connect phone…* under *Other actions*; when the research is not running, it offers to start it first. It says what appears on the computer, turns the connection on through a window on the computer (or names the terminal command) and shows a QR code for one phone with how long it is valid, and the other network when the computer has two. *Paired phones* lists each phone with when it was last seen, *Unpair* has *Undo*, and a phone paired again offers to unpair its older entry. When the phone has not reached the computer after a minute, a short checklist appears; *Turn connection off* stops it, and the menu and a dot on the *Research* button show while it is on.
- **What will be sent**: besides the people, the list shows the changed sources (with what changed and where they are cited), couples, places with new coordinates and surname variants, each in its own section; a long section shows the first few and expands on request. The count on the *Send* button includes these changes too. An *Undo* pill no longer covers the list's *Close* and *Send* buttons.
- **Clearer menus**: *Actions* (on a phone or tablet *More*) lists what is used most: *Anniversaries*, *Sources*, *Export…*, *For the family ›* (*Family book*, *Poster…*, *Slideshow (TV)*), *Tree: … ›* and, last, *Settings*, now in this one place instead of the toolbar gear. The *Research* menu shows its state in a compact block and at most six actions; the rest opens in place under *Other actions*. *Tree ›* is one list in groups, with splitting, merging and duplicating folded under *Change structure*. *Import…* is in the tree switcher. In the view mode, *Actions* has *Anniversaries*, *For the family ›*, *Tree overview* and *Settings*. *About Strom* is a link at the foot of *Settings*.
- **Source images from the research**: when the research leaves a source's images out of its file, opening the source loads them from the research running on this computer (research 1.18.0). They are kept with the tree in this browser, apart from its data, and *Remove from the app* takes them away again. *Sources* has the filter *Image in research* with *Load all from the research…*: counts, size and free space first, then progress with pause and stop. The *Research* menu row *Images in the research · N* opens *Sources* on that filter and shows the progress. The export offers *Source images*: none, only those in the app, or all from the research.
- **Checking the agent's work**: *What was written ›* groups the changes by type, and what the research does not read is marked *app only*. Sources in the research history are links that open the source read-only. A source whose image is not in the app says why beside its load button.
- **Fixes**: switching to *Descendants* right after zooming in with + no longer leaves the tree off-screen. In a tight toolbar the tree name gives way after the search field, so it is no longer cut to its first letter, and a renamed tree shows its new name at once. On a Mac, *Link to a file…* in Export names ⌘S instead of Ctrl+S. At the edge of a research tree, a child with only one parent in the tree shows the stub of the missing father or mother again, on that parent's side. A file sent to relatives without a name reads *A relative sent a family tree*, and the name field is no longer prefilled with a placeholder, not even one remembered by an earlier version. *Written, N conflicts* no longer comes back after the research is updated or its agent works on, once the research's version has been loaded; its count covers only the conflicts left open by the changes just sent. An emoji or another character outside the basic range (🔴, 🟢, 👨‍👩‍👧) in a long source title, note or place is no longer damaged into ‘��’ on its way to the research or in a GEDCOM export.
- **Dates in English** name the month, day first, as GEDCOM writes them: *15 May 1880*, *May 1880*, *about 15 May 1880*, also in the anniversaries of the tree overview (*15 May*). An all-numeric *3/4/1850* read differently in the US and in Britain. Typing a date works as before (*5/15/1880* is still read month first), and the edit fields keep *1880-05-15*.

## 3.13.1

*Released 19 October 2026.*

- **Tree overview**: *Births without a source* and *Line ends* count among the chosen person's ancestors (*3 of 14 ancestors*), the date of death is labelled as counted among the deceased, each birth month column is named with its count (a tap shows it on touch), percentages follow the language of the app, and after a change in the data the keyboard focus returns to the same control.
- **Tree overview, Data quality**: a tree that is not open offers *Validation details*; a finding names the recurring godparent (*Godparent: …*) or the place written several ways (*Place: …*); links in the overview no longer leave a space before a comma; the certainty segments are separated by a thin gap, with new dark-mode shades.
- **Research trees**: in *Ancestors*, a birth graded by the research counts as documented only when the research has proven it, as in the research's own statistics; a note under the table says that the imported family tree is a lead. The births *Where to focus* lists as not documented follow the same rule (*Births not proven: 3 people*), and a birth the research grades offers *Sources* instead of *Cite*. A hypothesis version without a link reads *no link to people in the tree*.
- **Jumping to a section**: *Data quality* from the tree actions or the research overview, and *Ask the family*, bring the heading fully into view under the overview's header.
- **Czech relationship terms**: a sibling with one shared parent is a *polorodý bratr* or *polorodá sestra*, in the tree overview and in *Find relationship…*.
- **Live view**: the directions heading counts the active directions (*Directions · 3 active*), and the person menu calls a running direction *already active*.
- **Research in other trees**: the tree switcher marks every research tree on this computer (*live*, *2 directions*, *3 waiting*, *not running*), and when closed it carries a dot for the other trees. The research panel and *Now* in the research overview name another tree where an agent works or something waits (*Elsewhere*); selecting it switches there. A tree whose research does not answer says *Research not running right now*, what waited last and how it reconnects.
- **Switching to a tree where an agent works**: the tree is followed live at once, with no question; unsent changes of the open tree are sent first, and the tree left becomes editable again. The *Live research* panel names the followed tree in its head and says briefly which tree is followed now and which stays editable. A tree where nobody works stays editable with *Follow live* offered, and its research panel says how edits reach the research: by themselves, with *Send*, or not at all when the research version is only loaded.
- **Directions at work**: when two or more research directions work at once, the *Live research* panel and *Now* in the research overview list each one in a row with its task and since when. Selecting a direction highlights its people in the tree and shows only its *Latest changes* and *Up next*, while *Awaiting action* stays whole and the other directions keep running. A selected direction that stops reads *Not at work now* with the time it was last seen; *All directions* or a second click drops the selection.
- **Fixes**: two quick changes in a row no longer show two Undo notices at once; names on the cards are fitted again once the card fonts are in, so a long name is no longer clipped.

## 3.13.0

*Released 17 October 2026.*

- **Edge of the tree**: a hypothesis whose versions would each link a different family outside the tree shows all of them. The bubble at the edge of the tree and *Above the person* list every family with its versions, people and waiting tasks, each a link to that family; the pill reads *+ 2 families* and offers each one. With Strom Research 1.16 or later.
- **Research for this tree**: one sentence says where the terminal the research opens in is chosen: in the research's settings, with *Settings in the research* beside it. On a computer, with Strom Research 1.16 or later, which brings the choice itself (Terminal or iTerm2 on a Mac).
- **Married surname**: a GEDCOM import keeps the surname after marriage (a second `NAME` with `TYPE married`, or `_MARNM`) apart from the birth surname instead of turning it into another spelling of the name, writes it back on export and to the research, and search finds the person under it too. Cards, the card menu, the person panels and search show the married surname with the birth surname as a quiet note (*née Dvořáková*); *Settings → Person card → Surname display* switches to the birth surname or the married one alone. The family book's index lists the person under both surnames, and *Surname spellings* shows who carries a surname as a married one, beside counts that stay with birth surnames. The person editor has a *Married surname* field under the surname (always there for a woman with a union, otherwise behind *+ married surname*), and under the empty field a suggestion from the husband's surname, in the feminine form where Czech rules apply (*Novák → Nováková*): *Use* fills it in, × hides it and the same suggestion is not offered again. A research version that brings back a married surname emptied here shows it as a row in *Load the research version?*
- **Tree overview**: one overview replaces *Statistics* and *Tree health*. *Where to focus* names what is worth doing next, with a step to fix or show it; *Ancestors* counts known and documented ancestors by generation and follows the four grandparent lines to where they end; *Life and health* (age at death), *Family through time*, *Data quality* and *Highlights* fill in on expanding. It opens from the tree actions, the tree manager, the person count in the focus bar and the research overview, and on a phone as a full-screen sheet. *Ask the family* under *Ancestors* lists the ancestors and their siblings who died in the last 100 years with missing details (date, place or cause of death, place of birth), each with the relation to the chosen person (*father’s father’s brother*), since archives mostly do not publish these records yet; a *Question for relatives* is saved to the person. In research trees, *Data quality* shows how certain the birth, death and marriage facts are according to the research, and the tree menus offer *Data quality* as a shortcut under *Tree overview*.
- **Merging a tree**: married surnames from a file (for example from MyHeritage) fill in the tree, and a married surname that differs on the two sides asks which one to keep, like the birth surname. A person with an unknown given name who stands in the same place in the family as one person in the tree (same sex and surname, dates that agree) is offered as a yellow match in *Review matches* instead of being added a second time.
- **Parent lines**: lines between parents and children no longer cross or run along the lines of another family. Where a line could not be drawn without that, it is left out and the cards link to each other instead: the child's card carries *parents ↗*, each parent's card *child ↗*, and a click brings the other end into view without changing the focus. The poster and the family book name the other end on those cards.
- **Fixes**: the *+ family · N* pill at the edge of the tree counts the family it shows: for a hypothesis whose variants would each connect a different family outside the tree, the number of people and waiting tasks now belongs to the family that appears on a click.
