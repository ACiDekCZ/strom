## 3.28.0

*This version is in preparation; this section grows with every test version.*

- **Views in their own windows** (on a computer, also one with a touch screen): ⧉ beside the view switch, ⧉ in the Table's bar, Ctrl/⌘+click, a middle click or the context menu on a view tab open *Family*, *Descendants*, *Timeline*, *Fan*, *Map* or *Table* in a separate window, for example on a second screen, as many as needed, while the main window stays on its view. A new window takes over the person in the centre and what the view shows (the Table's rows, filters, search, saved view and sorting; the zoom of the family view; the fan's rings; the map's places and zoom). Each window has its own view switch and ⧉ for another window. Edits made there are saved by the main window, go into its *Undo* and show in every window at once; the selected person is shared between the windows: a *Family*, *Descendants* or *Fan* window moves its centre to a person chosen in another window, the other views mark the person (*Follow selection*: switch, mark only or off, for each window). While research is followed live the windows are read-only; when the main window is closed they keep the last state read-only and offer *Open Strom* or *Make this the main window*. ⧉ shows how many windows are open, brings one to the front or closes them all; a window the browser blocked says so with *Try again*.
- **A second tab of the app** no longer opens a full second copy that could overwrite the list of trees and the settings: it says Strom is already open and offers *Switch there* or *Open as another window*; links opened in it (a person, a search, research links) go to the main window once. The window title names the open tree.
- **Phone and tablet**: the bottom bar reads Family | Descendants | + | *Table* | More, the *Timeline* moved to More beside *Fan* and *Map*. On a phone the legend sits at the bottom edge and back / forward stand above it (family view, fan). More offers *Full screen* where the browser allows it; Safari on an iPhone gets the way to the Home Screen instead. A slideshow keeps full screen that was on before it.
- **Table**: pinned columns can be put in any order, *Name* need not be first; a move that cannot be made is no longer offered, and *filled* stands in the first column.
- **Fixes**: *Research overview* opened without following closes again with ⤡ (and ×): the research's panel no longer lies over the button after following ended. *Stop following* in the overview only ends following; the overview stays open. Switching to another view closes the overview opened without following. *What will be sent* opened right after reloading the page shows the same list as before, not *The exact list of changes cannot be told here right now*.

## 3.27.0

*Released 12 November 2026.*

- **Table view**: a new view beside *Fan* and *Map* (in *More* on a phone and a tablet) shows people, unions, events or sources as a table with columns to choose, computed ages, completeness, the checks' findings, filters, search, sorting and saved views, and exports the rows to CSV or XLSX. A cell that stands for one fact is edited in place (double click or F2), a cell copies with Ctrl/Cmd+C, a filter can keep or leave out one value, the findings of a person open from their cell, and the person in focus goes along between the table and the tree. More cells are edited in place: the cause and the recorded age at death, the *Ref. number*, the partners' recorded ages at the wedding and the divorce's date and place; the sex and the union's type are chosen from their values, and an empty wedding or divorce cell on a union's row adds that fact. A union without a wedding has “–” in the wedding's cells, left out of the completeness. Beside *Full name* there is a *First name* column, edited in place; the *Coverage row* under a completeness column shows the average; a cell's menu opens at the cell; unsaved changes of views come back after a page reload; the *On this day* card stands at the bottom centre; and the table shows no focus history arrows (↩ ↪). It handles trees of thousands of people and grew out of a spreadsheet by Ondřej Chrpa.
- **Research in the Table view**: in a tree linked to a research the hidden group *Research* adds columns for what awaits a decision, conflicts, hypotheses, the AI agent's work, tasks, materials and the lines; *Research ▾* filters by them, ready views open them, and a click on a research cell opens a bubble with the details and their actions. While the research is followed, the table changes in place without its rows jumping.
- **Research hypotheses decided in the app**: with a research that allows it, *What the research knows*, *Awaiting action* and the bubble of a hypothesis in the tree offer *Choose* at each version, *No version holds* and *Leave open*, the AI agent's proposal can be accepted, also for several hypotheses at once, and a decision can be changed or reopened. The research's terminal confirms the choice, and the tree then loads the research's new version. A hypothesis whose versions link nobody in the tree (such as where a person came from) is decided the same way: the tree does not change, only the decision is recorded.
- **Data quality and dates**: new checks for a parent's age at a child's birth, a wedding under 16 and a recorded age more than two years off the dates; warnings are called *to check* everywhere. “about 1850”, “after 1850”, ranges such as “1850-1852” and a month such as “07/1987” are read as dates, the text staying as written. *Tree validation* calls its group *To check* and says under *Fix all (n)* what it fixes.
- **Order of marriages and spacing**: a marriage without a wedding date is placed by its divorce or its first child's birth; marriage numbers, bubbles, the table and the tree follow this order, the partners standing from left to right in the order of the marriages. *Settings → Person card* offers *Spacing*, *Normal* or *Dense*, so that more people fit on the screen; image export, poster and PDF print have their own choice. On the *Normal* and *Detailed* card a long name wraps onto more rows instead of ending in “…”.
- **Research overview and marks**: *Research overview* is the first item of the *Research* menu for every tree with a research, also without following and on a phone; it no longer replaces or locks the tree. With the research running it shows its work, the queue, the latest changes and the directions, which can be managed; with the research not running it says so with *Turn on* and shows *Awaiting action* as last known, the directions from the tree and *What the research knows*. *Follow live* is the second item and a button in the overview, which says that the tree then switches to the research version and cannot be edited; the overview never switches by itself when the agent starts. The *Table view* shows the agent's work and the queue without following too, the tree overview has *Research ›*, and the queue marks a story or a history task. *Customize panel* now also sets the order of the sections and, under *At the top*, which parts of *Last 24 h*, *This month* and *Follow in* show; the cost is hidden until switched on. A card where more awaits at once (a task, a conflict, a question) shows its marks stacked, the most pressing in front; their bubble lists each one, and a click offers what to open.
- **Where an entry stands in a source**: the source editor has *Archive* and *Call number*, then *Folio/page*, *Image* and *Other location*; an older location naming a folio or an image is proposed under the fields with *Use*, a location naming another folio or image than its field says so, and *Another entry from the same register* keeps the book. The source viewer names the *Book* and the *Entry*; *Sources*, the citation chip's tooltip, the person page and the *Family book* name the call number, folio and image as well, an older source reading as before. In the *Table view*, *Sources* shows *Call number*, *Folio/page* and *Image* by default and *People* offers them in *Columns*; the research's column of where the scan is is now called *Scan*. *Split as suggested…* in *Sources* and in the *Table view* splits the older locations of all sources at once, with a preview and one step of *Undo*: in a tree without a research in the app, in a research tree through the research while it runs; in a research tree its preview shows the research's examples of locations not recognised. A long source title without spaces wraps in *Sources* and in the source viewer. GEDCOM reads and writes the call number under the archive (`REPO > CALN`) as *Call number* instead of a note line, writes the folio and the image into `PAGE` for other programs and keeps the fields in `_STROM_FOLIO`, `_STROM_IMAGE` and `_STROM_LOCATOR`. Trees of earlier versions open without a change.
- **Fixes**: at an address without https the password dialogs say that encryption works only over https or on this computer, and a tree with photos saves there; *Whole families* no longer stops with an error when a child's marriage joins the parents' marriages; in *Read a document* a match resting only on an uncertain name is preselected on *No*; the dot on *Actions* goes out once *Anniversaries* has been opened; the *Table view*'s status line says *Following the research live* only while the research is followed, and without following names the running research its agent and tasks come from; a source cited at two facts with a different reliability (the occupation read as *Original*, the age as *Copy / index*) is one source in *Sources* again, with its image, and the reliability is shown at each citation; trees saved in 3.26 get such sources joined back on opening, with a backup first. GEDCOM: pictures of persons and families from another program's file go back with *Back to {program}*; merging two persons by hand keeps the notes of both; an id that several persons carry only offers a match; when *Download photos from the internet* gets no photo, the message says that the photos could not be downloaded.

## 3.26.0

*Released 9 November 2026.*

- **GEDCOM from Windows programs**: a file from a Central European Windows program (*CP1250*, *ANSI* or without a stated encoding) is read with its accented letters intact. The import summary shows sample names under *Text encoding*; when letters look garbled (è instead of č), another encoding can be chosen and the file is read again. ANSEL files also read the LDS extension characters, the eszett and the euro sign.
- **Where facts came from**: a tree from a GEDCOM file remembers which file each fact came from (program, file date). Each fact shows one reliability mark, *Confirmed*, *Proven*, *From family tree* or *No source*, and a tap says what the mark means. The person card says once where its facts came from, and the cards in the tree show the weakest mark. The pill at a wedding and a divorce stays beside its label, and a pill's explanation opens under its own row, on screen. With a research that supports it, a fact the research changed shows *Originally from the family tree* with the earlier value and *Revert*. The research's proposal to change a fact from the family tree or entered by a person sits at the fact with *Keep the family tree's* and *Accept the proposal*. A file export keeps where each fact came from also for a fact without a date or a place (a death known only as a death, a wedding, an event with only a note), and an age the research wrote at a birth goes back to it as an age, not as a note. Loading the research's version of a tree read before no longer lists changes that are none: the same sources, citations and events read again stay as they were, and a citation row names its page. Following a research live shows its last built tree at once, with *Preparing a newer version…* until the newer one replaces it, and a tree that has not changed is not downloaded again. After a fact changes, only the tree comes again, without its images: only images the app does not hold yet are downloaded.
- **One mark on the card**: a tree card carries one mark in its bottom-right corner for how well the person is backed and who stands behind the facts, instead of the evidence stripes and a second small mark. A research tree gets its marks right after a page load, with the research's bridge and without it; a value the research took over from the family tree keeps the family tree's mark, never the confirmed one. The mark measures every person the same way: of name, birth and death only those with a reliability step count and the weakest decides (confirmed by a person ✓, proven ●● however many readers, probable or possible with a citation of an original or a copy with primary information ●○, otherwise ○), a person whose name, birth and death carry no step is marked by the other facts, and the legend reads the same in every tree: confirmed by a person, documented, partly documented, only a trace; ○○ stays for a fact from a family tree file and a dotted frame for no source; the bubble says the mark in words, the tree legend names only the marks the tree shows, the mark stays readable zoomed far out without covering the card's text and goes into print and the image export with the story's fold; *Settings → Show on card* has every card mark in three groups (*Person status*, *Awaiting a decision*, *Links*), all on, earlier choices carried over; the ⇄ pill counts the other trees a person is in, not the matches; a phone held sideways shows the person menu as a panel at the right; a card with year ranges at birth and death keeps both years whole. Pointing at a mark on a card (the status mark, the badge awaiting a decision, the agent's arc, the lock, the story's corner, ⇄ and the sibling order) shows a bubble that says what it is and what a click does; by touch a tap on a mark opens its bubble with the action as a button. The status mark and every reliability pill at a fact open one status bubble: the step, the facts it covers, the sources cited at the fact as links that open the source at that citation (three, then *and N more ›*) and *What the marks mean*. The person menu no longer starts with a block explaining the marks, only with what there is to do (*Answer*, *What the research knows*, *Follow the research*, *Go to the tree*). The help draws the reliability marks only with ● and ○, as the app shows them, and describes *Confirm against the scan* and following a research live.
- **Verify facts**: with a research that supports it, the status bubble of a probable, possible or family-tree fact offers *Verify*: a preview says what is checked, how (reading a known record, or finding the record by the family tree's citation, which costs more) and the estimate; after *Start verifying* the pill shows a small clock and the place in the research's queue. *Verify facts · N* in the person menu and on the card checks all of a person's facts, with a preview per fact; *Verify facts in the tree…* (in *What the research knows*, the research menu and the *What next* card after an import) checks a line, a family or the whole tree, nearest ancestors first and on 5 people first, and *Stop* cancels what has not started. The bubble says why a fact stayed probable after a check, and when two readers agreed. *Confirm against the scan* marks a cited fact as confirmed by a person (✓), also without a research, with *Undo*; confirmations go to the research with the tree. *Confirm against the scan* is offered only at a fact with a source and confirms only the value the research holds now: a fact changed meanwhile confirms nothing, says why and offers the research's current version. The preview names each fact as the app does elsewhere, and a check or a confirmation the research refuses says why (busy, held by another session, an archive, a withdrawn fact, an older research). After a research update a message says once how many facts with an original wait to be verified. A confirmation taken back in the app also reaches the research through a GEDCOM file of the research's tree.
- **What next after an import**: after a GEDCOM from another program a *What next* card shows where the people came from, the data check, the families nothing connects with the rest and ancestor research with this tree, once per import. Inserting a GEDCOM into an open tree saves a version *Before import* first.
- **Export by purpose**: a GEDCOM export asks *What the file is for*. *Back to {program}* (for example *Back to MyHeritage*) carries the whole tree with every source and citation, the research's facts optionally marked with a note; *To share with others* protects living people while the deceased keep their sources. The footer says how many sources and citations a choice leaves out, with *Keep sources*. The German note about living people in the export no longer addresses the reader.
- **Ids and tags of other programs kept**: the ids another program gave its people and families, and its own tags at a person or family, stay in the tree, and *Back to {program}* writes them back; the import summary names the kept tags. Media records linked from people and sources are read, and the face cut-outs MyHeritage writes under photos no longer count as unsupported. *Back to {program}* also gives back the tags of source records, a photo's date, people without a name with their families, e-mail and name parts, adds no marriage the file did not have, and a child of two families keeps both, also when the second family has one parent; tags kept for the way back no longer count as unsupported, and the import offers images only when the file has some. A source record cited at many pages goes back as that one record with its author, each citation with its own page, quality and date; a citation without a page no longer takes another citation's page. A family of one spouse that the file keeps as a record stays with its ids. A picture linked by path or address, with no file taken in, goes back as it was written, with its date. A name after marriage with given names of its own (*Jana Kateřina Nováková* beside *Jana Dvořáková*) keeps them: shown with the married surname, and every export and the research get the name as it came. *Back to {program}* and the file read again also keep links to media, a citation's text and role, dates and ages Strom cannot read, a note on a divorce and details without a field of their own (a wedding's type, the number of children, a note under a birth) in their original form, and a source record cited nowhere keeps its id. A couple written as two family records (divorced in one, married again in the other) reads as one couple, the import summary counts such records, and *Back to {program}* writes both again, each with its own children, notes, citations and date. Text another program writes on a fact's own line (education, burial, a custom event), an address written in parts (ADR1, CITY…), a web link in a source's text (MyHeritage) and a divorce's citations with their quality and text are kept and go back as the file had them, and merging no longer folds a citation into one at another quality. A divorce some programs (PAF) write before the wedding no longer makes the couple married, and a given name a file writes as a description (*Son*, *Unknown*) goes back as written, without a note made of it. A wedding or a divorce written empty ahead of a dated one (MyHeritage) keeps the dated one's date and place. What an earlier version did not keep at import comes back only by importing the file again, or by merging it into the tree (unedited facts get it; such a divorce is offered under *Partnership conflicts*).
- **Merging**: merging the same file again no longer doubles notes, only what the tree does not say yet is added; it also fills the e-mail of facts and the couples without a marriage in the file that an earlier import did not keep, and people without a name pair up by their id in the file. Merging the same file into a tree loaded by an older version gives the same *Back to {program}* as a fresh import, and finding the pairs takes seconds on a file of a few thousand people. Only matches scoring 80 or more confirm themselves, 50 to 79 are offered for review. People with the same id in both files pair up as *Same ID in the file*, when their names, sex and birth years agree. Siblings known only by their surname are no longer added a second time when the same file is merged again; duplicates from an earlier merge stay and are removed by hand. Merging the file into a tree an older version loaded no longer doubles a family with one named parent. A large tree opens and redraws without freezing when many other trees are stored.
- **Smaller changes**: the card's *!* and the person menu's *Answer · N waiting* answer: with one item waiting for the person its answer opens, as its *Answer* in the panel does; with more, the research panel opens at the person's items, first and highlighted (the overview on a phone); an item that cannot be answered here is highlighted with the reason. In *Back to {program}* the line *Sources and citations* counts the source records the file writes: one register cited at many pages is one source there, while the tree keeps one source per cited page. A fact the research queued by itself further down its queue offers *Verify first* in its bubble, with the same preview and estimate as *Verify*, and the person menu's *Verifying · N* offers it for the whole person.

## 3.25.0

*Released 8 November 2026.*

- **Research on**: with a research that supports it, the research panel and *Manage trees* have a switch *Research on* for each research tree, showing whether its research runs on this computer. Switching it off ends the research's connection until it is switched on again; switching it on starts it quietly, without a window or a terminal. The panel then says whether it was switched off or went off after a time without work, which new work in the tree switches on again. *Settings* has a new group *Research on this computer* with the choice *Until the computer shuts down* or *After 2 hours without work*, for all trees on this computer. With an older research the switch only shows the state, and the panel and *Settings* give the command for the terminal. A phone shows no switch.
- **Reading a document with other agents and models**: with a research that offers it, *Read a document* says who reads (program, model and thoroughness), and *Adjust reading* chooses in plain words the program, model and *Thoroughness* of each reader, for example another AI agent as the second reader. The choice goes to the research only when it differs from its settings; an agent that cannot read now (limit reached, sign-in expired) is said with the reason and the reading does not start. The progress, the result, the research panel and the list of readings say who read, and where two readers differ each version carries its reader's mark. On a phone each choice is a full-width field. With a research that sends the second reader's own text, *Reader 1* and *Reader 2* above the transcript switch between the two transcripts, each with the places marked. Readers that differ even on the kind of entry (a marriage against a baptism) are said at the result and before *Suggest for the tree…*, to be checked against the scan. Under the scan *Full quality* (where the research keeps the original) and *Open the page in the archive* open the original; an entry of one line (a register row) lights up its frame instead of a line strip. A model that always takes the newest version (*Opus*) says, where the research knows it, which version it runs on now (*Opus (now Opus 5.5)*); the choice stays the model itself, so a newer version is taken by itself. A Codex model the installed Codex may not know carries a note with the command for a newer Codex, and a reader whose agent turns its model down ends with the research's reason and what helps.
- **The reading result at a glance**: the result of *Read a document* and *Read again…* starts with one sentence (read, how many words are illegible, how many substantive changes and minor details against the existing transcript, whether the readers agree), and under it *Text as read* carries one mark per place, each kind in its own shape: a disagreement between the readers, a change against the existing transcript (only in reader 1's text), an uncertain word and an illegible place. *To check* lists the places by kind with the minor details (spelling, punctuation, abbreviation…) folded with their count; a place opens a bubble with both readings, the existing transcript and *Show on the scan*. The scan lights up the chosen line (approximately where the research does not say where the lines lie) and frames the source entry and the neighbouring entries, folded under *+ N neighbouring entries*; on a computer *Enlarge beside the text* keeps the enlarged scan beside the text. An entry the research could not match with the source offers *Compare with the existing transcript* in the app, with nothing saved. The footer says what became of the transcript. While reading, each reader has its own row (queued, reading, done or failed); a failed reading offers *Details* and *Try another model*, and a reading where one reader could not read says so and shows no disagreements. On a phone the chosen entry stands in a strip over the text with a *Scan* tab, and a place opens as a bottom sheet.
- **Reading a source again from its original**: with a research that keeps a source's original, the source viewer offers *Read again…* (AI) under the transcription, and beside the image for a source of one excerpt. The research reads the original again in full quality and compares the new reading with the transcription: the result says whether it matches or how many places differ. A difference is not a conflict; the transcription and its confirmation stay as they are. After a new reading with a substantive change the footer of the result chooses what follows: *Check with the agent now* (a conversation with the agent opens in the terminal), *Queue for the agent* or *Just save*; at a transcript a person confirmed, *Set aside for review* creates a task for a person, and the agent does not change the transcript. Only minor details, or places where the readers merely disagree, create no task by themselves; the uncertain places can be handed over on request with *Give to the agent to assess* (the agent corrects the transcript only where the scan is clear), at a transcript a person confirmed as a proposal to assess. *To check* sorts the places into *Substantive changes to check*, *Uncertain – readers disagree*, *Already decided: kept* and *Minor details*. The source then says the state of its newer reading (not checked, saved, task queued, assessed) and offers *Decide* and *Give to the agent…*. The agent's proposal for a confirmed transcript shows its places against the transcript and *Whole text side by side*, with *Accept the proposal*, *Keep the current one* and *Undo*. *Research for this tree* has the group *Reading documents* with *Task from a new reading*: *Ask*, *Always create* or *Never create*. On a phone the viewer says that reading again works in the app on the computer with the research. The sentence on top of the result says how the new reading compares with the existing transcript, and the footer says the transcription stays as it is. With a research that says which entry of the excerpt is the source's, that entry is chosen first and the others are marked *neighbouring*, not compared with the transcription. A comparison that did not succeed says so instead of the count, and a result the research could not write down says so beside the comparison.
- **Confirming the research's transcription**: with a research that supports it, the source viewer offers *Confirm transcription* at the end of the line *Transcription read by the research*. The confirmation is written to the research at once and the line then says the transcription was confirmed in the app, with the day; *Undo confirmation* takes it back with one click. While the research is not running the line says confirming is possible once it runs, and a transcription the research changed meanwhile is said under the line, to be confirmed once the new wording arrives. Confirming works also while the research is followed: the confirmation goes to the research and does not change the tree. The app's GEDCOM export keeps who read a transcription.
- **Who decides hypotheses**: with a research that supports it, *Research for this tree* chooses *The user decides* or *The AI decides*. *The user decides* is written to the research at once; *The AI decides* is confirmed in the research's terminal, and switching does not change hypotheses already decided. In *What the research knows* a hypothesis the agent decided says *decided by the agent* with the day and its reason (a long reason unfolds), and a hypothesis offers *Decide in the research* or, once decided, *Change in the research*, where the research on this computer offers it.
- **Looking up places in the research from the map**: with a tree that has a research, the map's status bar offers *In the research* (AI) beside the map's own lookup. It opens the same preview and *Queue* as *Places*, for the places without a position the research is not working on yet, and nothing is queued before *Queue*. When the map's lookup does not find some places, the bar says so and offers looking them up in the research; a followed place without a position offers it too. With the research on another computer or on a phone, the button says where it is done.
- **Screen readers**: closed dialogs and menus are no longer read out, icon buttons (zoom, filters, back and forward, photo rotation and others) have a readable name, a bottom sheet takes no taps while it slides in, and with reduced motion menus, notices and the bottom sheet appear without animation. *How Strom works* has a new chapter *Using the app*: where things are in the app, what the controls are called and editing while following the research.
- **Smaller changes**: the results of *Find on the map* lie where the tree lives, with results far away in another country folded under *More far away (N)*; a place marked *Check* in *Places* offers *Show on the map* beside *Keep* and *Change*, and a far or other-district warning adds *Possibly another place of the same name.*; while the research is followed, the person menu shows *Edit* greyed with why and *Stop following* right under it; the *?* in forms and in the research's dialogs opens *How Strom works* at the rule of that field; the filter in *Sources* reads *Image only in the research*; in the life timeline a former partner's line ends at a dated divorce or separation; the hollow marker on the map is named *no person on the map yet*; *Read a document* says when the agent's terminal program is missing; a conflict's value no longer shows a leftover source label such as *@s:*; the *Research on* switch no longer stays greyed after a research update (greyed only where the research said it cannot switch on), and research that is off offers *Switch on* in the research panel with the one command `strom live start` under it; *What the research knows* offers, in one sentence, a switch to *The AI decides* in the research when a person decides and 10 or more hypotheses are open, and the offer can be hidden; *Read again…* names two parts of one page *Excerpt 1* and *Excerpt 2*, each view frames the part it read, and the result says in how many places the independent reader 2 differed; the reading time said follows the slowest reader and, once past it, says the reading waits for the other reader; a changed choice of readers is kept for the next readings and *Default* forgets it at once; when the research confirms a started reading late (busy at that moment), the dialog says so and follows the reading once the research lists it, instead of going back to the input with *Read* offered again; *Another entry from the same register* prefills only the book and page, without the entry's number and date; in the dark theme, text on the green fill (active segments, badges, the open help mark) is easier to read.

## 3.24.0

*Released 6 November 2026.*

- **How Strom works in the app**: the whole documentation of the app and the research, in English, Czech, German and Polish, is part of the single file and opens without a connection, from *Actions*, *More* on a phone and *About Strom*. Contents by part (A · R · S), search by a word or a rule number (*R4.3*), a *Glossary*, links between rules with *Back*, and *On the web ↗* for the same page on the website. A rule newer than the research connected to the tree says so.
- **Help right at the rule**: a quiet *?* in the headings of *Queue*, *Awaiting action* and the agent's work in the *Research overview* and its small panel, of *Places*, *Places in the tree*, the conflicts panel and a person's *Conflicts* opens *How Strom works* at the rule that part follows.
- **Towns of the same name on the map**: when several towns share a place's name (Lhota), finding the place takes the one nearest the tree's other places instead of the first match, still with one request per place. The place's check says how many towns of that name the map found. Locations placed by hand or kept are never moved, neither by a lookup nor by a merged file.
- **Translation of a transcript**: a source whose note carries the research's translation of the transcript (of a Latin or Cyrillic register entry, say) shows it under the transcript in the source view and on the person page, folded as *Translation of the transcript*. The note itself stays as it was.
